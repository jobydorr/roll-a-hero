# Progress Journal — The Folded World

*This is Joby's reference for where the campaign stands, written so that it can be picked up after a long gap without rereading the bible. Each entry is dated and the newest sits at the top. Every entry separates what is canon from what is only leaning or still open. ## 2026-10-15 — where we are, and what we are leading up to

### In brief

A wizard folded the world on behalf of alien partners, stopped halfway when he saw what it was for, and has held it jammed ever since, while the Surveyors rule kindly and harvest sunsalt. Green Fields has commissioned the party to go into the Fold after five people taken at Lastlight, and the next evening carries them from the night at the Keystone to their first fight inside the Fold. Beneath the rescue runs a thread that is not yet canon, in which someone inside the Fold is buying captives in order to draw sunsalt out of them.

### The world, as canon has it

The Foldwright, the most gifted wizard of his age, made a bargain with the Surveyors, beings from another dimension who offered an end to want and to distance. Folding the world produces sunsalt, which is the light of their own world steeping into ours, and the folding swallowed whole cities along with the world's memory of ever having had them. Partway through the working he understood that the fold was turning the world into an envelope addressed to something at its center, which the bible calls the Devouring, and so he jammed the working at its midpoint and fled into the Snarl with the Armature, the machine that drives the fold. He is still in there holding the jam, and the foldquakes are his grip slipping.

The Surveyors have ruled ever since, courteously and with soft hands, and they have said nothing at all about the man for three generations, so that his name survives detached from his deed. Two rebellions stand against them. Green Fields is the legal and popular one, and it is quietly steered from the top by Surveyors folded into human shapes. Red Flag is small, dark-windowed and right, and its article of faith, THE FOLDWRIGHT LIVES, is correct about the man and badly wrong about what finding him will bring.

The campaign's quietest crime is the Reactors. They replaced the old open Draws, and the damage they do did not stop but moved inside the Fold, where the lost places are going gray out of sight of anyone who would be believed. Tower workers come out faded, and they are sent off in white wagons to rest villages that are empty shells, while the faded actually go into holding-creases past the sealed doors on the Reactors' deepest floors. They are not dead and they can be found, but what is done to them there is still open (bible §8, Q17). One asymmetry in the substance matters for play: refined sunsalt folds and raw sunsalt unfolds, which is why the party's way in is raw sunsalt pressed into the socket of Ganny's object.

### The thread about people and sunsalt (not canon)

This thread lives on the back-burner page in the DM OS (`▶▶ BACK BURNER — the Fold`) and has not been ruled. Joby's idea is that sunsalt is a universal force, the raw energy of existence, and that people are one source among many who happen to yield an especially potent form of it. Somebody inside the Fold extracts it from people, by ritual and by instrument, and that somebody is the buyer to whom the goblins are delivering their captives.

Joby set a guardrail on the idea. Human-derived sunsalt is rare to the point of legend, it does not light lamps or stock potion shelves, and it is a specific ingredient awaiting a specific use that has not been decided. Extraction fades a person rather than killing them, so the rule against permanent death holds.

Three twists were put up against the obvious version, and Joby leans toward the third, in which sunsalt can be put back: a drained person restored, and dead gray land brought back to life. That would turn the finale into a real dilemma, because unfolding the world would cost the only cure for what has already been taken, and Joby wants it connected to the Foldwright in a way that is not yet designed. The two twists not chosen remain available. In the first, the potency comes from unfinished-ness rather than from being human, which would explain why a child yields more. In the second, a legal, honored and voluntary form of giving stands in front of the crime and undercuts any exposé.

The idea fits material already written, which is why it does not read as a retrofit. If adopted it answers Q17, since one of the candidates listed there is that the faded's threads are drawn off to sweeten the refining. Hettie's ledger already holds Reactor intake that never balances alongside a quiet census of the faded, and under this idea those are the same crime. The gray gains a meaning as well, because gray land becomes land that something was drained out of.

Three questions remain open: who the buyer is, which would be a new power and a real commitment; where the taken are kept, for which Joby's pick is a folded town run by goblins and other creatures, though it is not settled; and whether the Foldwright ever knew what sunsalt was.

### The taken (canon)

Five people were taken at Lastlight, Finch among them, and the table was told five. The hidden truth, settled on 2026-10-15, is that people have been taken the same way from other places, which the ministry has never disclosed and which hints that the folding is weakening. The goblins' tally reveals it with five knots, then four, then a few more, and where the other raids took place is Joby's to decide.

### Further out

The trips after this one sit on the back burner rather than in canon. The party rescues the five from the town on the face, then reaches the tower, and then finds the bay with twelve berths and one ship left, a ship that runs a fixed route rather than navigating the Snarl, which keeps the rule that only the Armature and the Compass Spirit can find their way through it.

Act One ends when the party learns that the gray is man-made, that the rest villages are shells, and that the movement they joined is on a leash, and its last image is a Green Fields officer coming undone into a drift of paper panes. The turn that hands the party to Red Flag used to rest on Tobin's filings. With Tobin cut, the proposal is that Mordrin discovers his own filings are steering the expeditions, and that waits on Brandon's answers. In Act Two the messiah on the walls turns out to be the man who signed the bargain and also the only thing still holding the world together. Act Three brings the Devouring at the center, the First Surveyor's sincere case for finishing the fold, and a Foldwright tempted by his family a single door away.

### Rulings and new thoughts from this entry

Healing is not outlawed, and standard D&D magic, gods and lore all hold in this world (Joby's ruling, 2026-10-15, not yet in the bible). The bible still says otherwise because its Menders section dates from an earlier design in which healing was an outlawed folk art guided by hearth-spirits instead of gods. When Tobin was made a full cleric, the outlawed layer was cut from him but never from the bible. Joby's reasoning is that the Surveyors are not threatened by magic or by other gods, because they believe their own power is superior to all of it.

A new thought, proposed and not yet canon, follows from that ruling. The Surveyors' confidence is earned, because they can switch magic off whenever they wish and fold anything out of their way, and since almost nothing threatens them, they can afford to permit almost everything the world held before they came.

### Loose ends

The bible's Menders section, rule 18 in §7, Q19 in §8 and the glossary entries for the menders, the Mending and the hearth-spirits all still describe outlawed healing, and they need rewriting to the ruling above once its shape is agreed. With Tobin gone, only Sam's Cure Wounds heals the party, which makes the patrol's two potions matter. Brandon's answers to his seven questions, the redesign of the Unfold-Rule portals into something more epic, and the location of the other raids are all still outstanding.
